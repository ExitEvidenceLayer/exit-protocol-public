# Drift, 1 April 2026: what the chain showed before the admin takeover

EXIT Protocol · 27 October 2026 · all times UTC

On 1 April 2026, admin control of Drift Protocol on Solana moved to a new address in two transactions four slots apart. BlockSec
estimates the loss at about $285 million. Published analyses describe governance transactions that two members of Drift's
Security Council were led to sign in advance and that were held with Solana durable nonces until the moment they were used
([BlockSec](https://blocksec.com/blog/drift-protocol-incident-multisig-governance-compromise-via-durable-nonce-exploitation),
[4pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/reflections-on-the-drift-protocol-exploit)).

We re-read the chain with one question: what was visible before the execution, and when?

## What the chain shows

Every item below was re-read from Solana mainnet on 27 October 2026.

- **The first transaction** (slot 410,344,005, 1 April 16:05:18) started with `AdvanceNonceAccount` on nonce account
  `7s7s6saC…`. Its nonce authority, `39JyWrdb…`, was the transaction's only signer. The transaction then created a Squads
  proposal on multisig `2LW6PSEj…` and approved it.
- **The second transaction** (slot 410,344,009, 16:05:19) started with `AdvanceNonceAccount` on nonce account `EmYEryTD…`,
  whose nonce authority, `6UJbu9ut…`, was again the only signer. It approved the same proposal and executed it. The execution
  called Drift's `UpdateAdmin`, which moved the admin from the multisig's vault 0 (`AiLGdNit…`) to `H7PiGqqU…`.
- **Both nonce authorities were member keys of the multisig.** They are the two approvals recorded on the executed proposal.
- **The nonce accounts were created days earlier:** `7s7s6saC…` on 24 March at 01:22:06, `EmYEryTD…` on 31 March at 02:35:49.
  Each account's history holds only its creation and its one use. The same account, `FMJnBkVp…`, created and funded both. (The
  analyses date the first nonce accounts to 23 March; the chain shows 24 March, 01:22 UTC.)
- **The multisig** was created on 25 March at 16:58:31 with five members, a threshold of two and a time lock of zero seconds.
  Its configuration stayed the same from its creation until the admin transfer: the settings set at creation match the account,
  and Squads' published source records every member, threshold or time-lock change by advancing a "stale" index that is still
  zero (assuming the deployed program follows that source). Transactions after the transfer are outside this statement. The
  multisig also has a configuration authority, a program-derived address, that can change members, threshold and time lock
  without a proposal.

## What this shows, and what it does not

- **In the takeover's own transactions, the only preparation that had been on chain beforehand was the two nonce accounts tied
  to member keys.** With a zero time lock, the multisig's proposal path left almost no window: proposal, approvals and execution
  fit into four slots, about a second.
- **A durable nonce account is a normal Solana feature,** and operators use them for legitimate reasons. A radar report states
  that a prepared state exists and which key it is tied to. It is not a verdict.
- **EXIT as it runs today would not have watched this multisig.** Our watches start from token mints and Ethereum contracts.
  Drift's admin authority sits in its program's state, and the DRIFT token mint has no mint or freeze authority, so no current
  watch leads to this multisig. Watching a multisig directly is the gap this case makes concrete, and it is the radar's next step.
- **Not covered here:** the signing itself, which happened off chain, and other nonce accounts the analyses mention. Two of those
  belong to accounts the analyses attribute to the attacker; a nonce account authorized to a key that is not watched is outside
  this kind of watch. We reconstructed only the two nonce accounts the takeover's transactions used; we did not scan every nonce
  account of that period. Nothing here claims the takeover could have been stopped.
